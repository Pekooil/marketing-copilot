# Pricing interview and commitment script

Run after the first credible Constraint Brief, and repeat after a completed learning loop where possible.

## Value before price

1. What decision did this workflow help you make?
2. What would you have done without it?
3. Which part was hardest to replace with analytics, generic AI, a spreadsheet, or a contractor?
4. How much founder time did it save, if any?
5. Would you choose to run this next week? Why or why not?

## Pricing sequence

Use the assigned offer/price; do not bargain during the first response.

“The offer would cost **${{49|99|199}} per month** for one workspace, including {{assigned package}}. Would you start a paid pilot at that price when the study ends?”

Record:

- yes now / yes with condition / maybe / no;
- reason in participant’s words;
- exact condition or missing capability;
- who approves payment and budget source;
- requested cancellation/trial terms;
- whether they will take a concrete next step.

## Commitment ladder

From strongest to weakest:

1. Paid pilot/payment authorization.
2. Signed or emailed agreement to start on a date.
3. Scheduled purchase decision with economic buyer.
4. Permission to send a checkout link.
5. Stated willingness only.

Do not count compliments, feature enthusiasm, or hypothetical “probably” as willingness to pay.

## Price sensitivity follow-up

Only after the assigned-price response:

- At what price would this feel too expensive to consider?
- At what price would you question whether it is credible/useful?
- Which capability would need to be present for the assigned price?
- Would you prefer a free diagnostic plus subscription, or a paid operating loop from day one?
- Does “AI Growth Lead” or “adaptive weekly growth plan” better describe what you would buy?

## Close

Explain that pricing and features are research hypotheses. Do not charge or promise launch timing without a separate authorized pilot agreement.
