# Pricing assignment and evidence plan

## Assignment

- Rotate $49, $99, and $199 across qualified participants rather than choosing price based on perceived wealth.
- Balance cells approximately across maturity and analytics availability.
- Rotate positioning A/B within price cells when sample permits.
- Preserve assignment even after objections; follow-up sensitivity questions come later.

With 8–12 participants, results are qualitative directional evidence, not statistical price optimization.

## Observation points

| Moment | Purpose |

## Analysis

- Counts by assigned price and commitment strength.
- Reasons for yes/no, grouped without erasing outliers.
- Difference between stated and concrete commitment.
- Value language participants repeat unaided.
- Capabilities whose absence blocks purchase.
- Whether commitment strengthens after adaptation.
- Segment/maturity differences, labeled exploratory.

## Bias controls

- Ask value questions before revealing price.
- Do not lead with build cost or desired price.
- Separate operator from note-taker/reviewer where possible.
- Record exact language, including negative reactions.
- Do not remove “bad fit” cases solely because they reject price.
